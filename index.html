PROPOSED WORKSHOP

AI Tools, Not “AI Friends”

Rethinking the Personification of Generative AI and Designing for Increased Agency and Reflection

Proposed workshop In person Submissions due Feb 10, 2027
01 — OVERVIEW

Can we design AI as something other than a “friend”?

Commercial AI platforms are typically presented as agents filling social roles such as assistant, friend, teacher, or coach. This workshop asks whether that framing serves the people using these systems, or whether it quietly costs them agency, reflection, and accountability.

This workshop brings researchers together to explore alternative metaphors, interaction paradigms, and design goals for genAI interfaces, and to develop a shared roadmap that better reflects HCI's values.

02 — GUIDING QUESTIONS

What the workshop sets out to answer

Four questions structure the day and its outputs:

Q1

What user-centered design goals should we promote as a community for genAI systems?

Q2

How should we define the role of genAI in human-AI collaboration?

Q3

How could we rethink the current genAI interaction design paradigm to meet those design goals?

Q4

How could designers de-personify genAI interfaces, and if so, what alternatives could serve users’ needs better?

03 — WORKSHOP DESIGN

Three activities, one shared roadmap

The session opens with 15–20 minutes of introductions to the topic and to each other, followed by three 40-minute activities.

Activity I · 40 min

Bringing in different design concepts

Design friction, seamful design, slow technology, and reflective design are starting points, not the full list. Participants first work individually to identify one HCI concept, method, or design approach that could productively challenge current AI interaction paradigms. In small groups they introduce their concepts, discuss how each might apply to genAI interface design, and document ideas on a shared canvas. Groups then choose the most promising concepts to carry forward, building a set of design lenses to use throughout the day and beyond.

Activity II · 40 min

AI metaphors beyond personhood

Small groups generate two to three alternative metaphors for genAI that don't frame it as a person, friend, coach, or teammate, drawing on tools, instruments, environments, non-human collaborators, or other conceptual models. Each group sketches or describes how its metaphors might shape the system's interface and interaction, then shares them with the full workshop to discuss opportunities, limitations, and unintended consequences.

Activity III · 40 min

Brainstorming design goals & best practices

Building on earlier concepts and metaphors, participants ask: what metrics of success should we care about? The organizers propose prioritizing user agency, reflection, appropriate reliance, and transparency over engagement or efficiency alone, whereas a “friend” framing tends to optimize for intuitive, fun, engaging interactions and domain-generality. Groups develop two to three concrete design goals, weigh tensions among them (such as agency vs. convenience, or reflection vs. efficiency), and propose interface practices to support them. Proposals feed a collective discussion that shapes a shared roadmap.

04 — AFTER THE WORKSHOP

What the workshop aims to produce

Output 1

A research agenda

Focused on AI that sets the right expectations and benefits the end user, rather than being efficiency-driven and accounting only for business needs.

Output 2

Building collaboration

Bringing together researchers interested in redesigning AI interactions and interfaces, and facilitating cross-institution collaboration.

Output 3

Best practices

Developing and publishing best practices for the design of AI systems, with interested participants invited to contribute.

As a follow-up, organizers and interested participants will publish a paper summarizing the workshop's outcomes, metaphors, and best practices identified by the HCI community. Organizers are also in discussions with journals about a special issue for the submitted short papers.

05 — CALL FOR PARTICIPATION

AI does not have to be humanized

This proposed workshop is an interactive, in-person working session of small-group activities, collaborative brainstorming, and synthesis of a roadmap. We welcome participants with interests in HCI, interaction design, human-AI interaction, or related areas. Applicants submit in one of two formats:

Short paper
4 pages, excl. references · single or multi-author

Discusses AI design paradigms, user perceptions of AI interfaces or interactions, or related topics. Accepted papers will be published on the workshop website.

Statement of interest
1–2 pages · single author

A brief statement of your interest in the topic and reasons for joining. This option is offered only if the short-paper track does not fill the workshop.

Submission deadline
February 10, 2027

Submit via Google Form

Accepted participants take part in two pre-workshop activities: studying a shared list of design methodologies and concepts compiled from all submissions, and introducing themselves in a shared working document so groups can form around common interests and expertise.

Questions? Contact nsabri@ucsd.edu.
